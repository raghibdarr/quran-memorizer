# Selection memo: al-Nawawi's Forty (stage A)

**What ships**: al-Arba'in al-Nawawiyya as al-Nawawi compiled it: his forty-two narrations, in
his order, numbered as he numbered them (`nawawi40`, "al-Nawawi's own order"). It is a named
classical work, so the deck follows it whole rather than our own choice; nothing is added,
dropped or reordered. Ibn Rajab's eight additions (43 to 50) are not part of this deck.

**Why this work first**: it is the most widely memorised hadith collection, short enough to
learn, and each narration is a principle the rest of the religion refers back to (al-Nawawi
says so in his preface). Its commentaries give us classical witnesses for the text, the
attribution lines, and the scholarly verdicts on the narrations outside the two Sahihs.

**What an item holds**

- `arabic`: from al-Nawawi's opening "عن …" to the end of the narration. His closing
  attribution line ("رواه البخاري ومسلم", "حديث حسن رواه الترمذي") is not repeated in the
  Arabic; it becomes `citations` (the collections he names, with numbers confirmed in two
  witnesses, plus the item's place in the Forty) and, where he gives one, a `gradings` entry.
- Where al-Nawawi adds a second wording ("وفي رواية لمسلم"), it is a `variants` entry with its
  own citation.
- Ayahs the Prophet ﷺ recites inside a narration (hadith 10) stay in the hadith's Arabic,
  because they are part of the narration as the witnesses give it; they are named in the note.
- `segments` end at sentence ends; every narration of more than one sentence has them.

**Gradings**: inclusion in al-Bukhari or Muslim is shown as such. Outside the two Sahihs the
grading is al-Nawawi's own verdict where he gives one, or the verdict he quotes (al-Tirmidhi's),
located in the Forty and in the source; where Ibn Rajab reports a located dissent, it is shown
beside it. Nothing is softened; a verdict we could not locate is not recorded.

**Status**: an item ships only after stages A to J of spec 5.4 pass. Items that could not be
fully checked stay `draft` and are not emitted; `review.log` says which and why.

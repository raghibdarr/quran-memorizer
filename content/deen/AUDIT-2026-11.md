## What was checked, and how

**Arabic against witnesses.**

- Adhkar and dua decks (45 non-Quran texts, variants included): `adhkar/tools/witness.mjs
  verify` on a fresh cache. Each text was found, by normalised skeleton, at the cited number
  in the Shamela copy of the printed edition the numbering names (not Sunnah.com-derived)
  and in hadith-api; where the two read differently the item's `provenance.note` says which
  was followed, and each such note was read against both texts (Muslim 591, al-Bukhari 6306,
  6311, 6320, 6346, 6382, Muslim 1342, Ibn Majah 3871). 0 problems.
- The eight Quran items store no Arabic. The hadith behind each use was read in both
  witnesses: Abu Dawud 5082 and al-Tirmidhi 3575 (the three surahs, three times, morning and
  evening), al-Bukhari 5017 (the three surahs at bedtime), al-Bukhari 2311 (Ayat al-Kursi;
  given by al-Bukhari without his own full chain, which the item says), al-Bukhari 5009 and
  Muslim 808 (the end of al-Baqarah).
- The Forty: `nawawi40/tools/collate.mjs --vowels` on a fresh cache against Ibn Daqiq
  al-'Id's commentary, al-Haytami's al-Fath al-Mubin and hadith-api. Every recorded hash
  was reproduced; every difference listed in a `provenance.note` is the difference the
  collation prints. The Arabic of all 15 was also read by eye.
- Fasting evidence hadith: al-Bukhari 1894 and 1933 and Abu Dawud 2357 and 2358 match both
  witnesses word for word.
- Names: the list and its order were read against al-Tirmidhi 3507; every cited ayah was
  read against the bundled text (`names99/check-names.mjs` and guardrail 6 are green).

**Citation numbers.** Every number on a hadith or dua item was confirmed in two witnesses
(Shamela print and hadith-api). For the Forty, al-Nawawi's wording is not always the
wording at the cited number (he names a collection, not a narration), so the check was that
the same hadith stands at that number: confirmed for all 27 numbers. Two notes: Muslim 1337
occurs twice in Abd al-Baqi's print, and hadith 9 is the second (Kitab al-Fada'il);
al-Bukhari 7288 and 6878 give the same hadith in different words.

**Gradings.** Every verdict with a quote was located again: al-Tirmidhi's remarks after
hadith 7, 464, 1858, 2518, 3388, 3391, 3392, 3426 and 3575 in Ahmad Shakir's print;
al-Nawawi's in al-Adhkar (nos. 71, 159, 192, 217); al-Daraqutni's after no. 2279 in the
Mu'assasat al-Risala print; Ibn Rajab's report on hadith 12 was read from memory of the
text only (see open doubts). Items in al-Bukhari or Muslim carry no other grading.

**Vowelling.** All 51 adhkar texts and variants and all 15 hadith of the Forty were
compared word by word with a vocalised witness. One difference in the adhkar decks
(hadith-api has a typing slip, لَمِنْ for لِمَنْ, at al-Bukhari 789; ours is right); in the
Forty, the three differences the items already record and explain (شقيٌّ أو سعيدٌ in
hadith 4, and two slips in al-Haytami's print).

**Translation.** Every English text in the decks was read against its Arabic, sentence by
sentence, for omissions, additions, tense and number, and loaded terms: all 15 hadith, all
45 adhkar texts and their variants, all 99 Names with their cited ayat, and every school
position in both course units against its stored Arabic quote. No error of meaning was
found. Notes of the Forty were read against Ibn Daqiq al-'Id's pages for hadith 4, 6, 7,
11, 13 and 14.

**Course units.** `course/purification/tools/verify-citations.mjs` re-fetched 73 pages and
found all 163 primer quotes, consensus citations and Bidayat al-Mujtahid cross-locations.
For Unit Fasting the primers' fasting chapters, Bidayat al-Mujtahid (Kitab al-Siyam) and
al-Ijma' were fetched again: 58 of 58 position quotes are on the pages recorded (one
crosses a page break in the second copy of al-Quduri and was joined by hand), and the
Ibn al-Mundhir numbers cited (68, 123 to 127, 129) say what the claims say. Every claim
of kind `differs` was read: each position says what its quote says and no more, no winner
is picked, and a school whose primer is silent is left out and the claim says so.

**Glossary, Essentials.** Each definition was read for accuracy. Essentials items carry
the same Arabic, English, citation and grading as their deck items; the counts after the
prayer (33, 33, 34: Muslim 596) and the daily hundred (Muslim 2702) match the hadith cited.

**Guardrails.** `node scripts/build-deen.mjs --check` is green, with one known false
warning (G8 on nawawi40 11: the name al-Hasan ibn Ali reads like the unmarked term hasan).

## What was changed

1. `nawawi40:07`, note. The printed commentary says Tamim al-Dari has no hadith but this
   one. Al-Nawawi's wording in his commentary on Sahih Muslim limits it to Sahih Muslim,
   and other collections relate hadith from Tamim. The sentence was removed from our
   paraphrase rather than repeated. rev 2.
2. `duas-daily:iftar`, grading reference. Al-Daraqutni's verdict had been read on one
   website only. It was located in the Shamela text of the Mu'assasat al-Risala print
   (shamela.ws/book/9771/2240, vol. 3 p. 156) and the reference now names that edition. rev 2.
3. `hijri-events:six-shawwal`. The description attributed positions to four imams with no
   source. Ibn Rushd lists the six fasts among the recommended fasts that are differed on
   and names Malik's dislike only; the line now says that and nothing else.
4. `zakat-notes`, two quotes from Bidayat al-Mujtahid (`debts`, `hawl`) each had a copying
   slip against the Dar al-Hadith print; corrected to the print. The English is unchanged.

## Open doubts for a scholar reviewer

1. **Everything in the two course units.** The positions are faithful to one short primer
   per school, which is not always the school's relied-upon view (for example the Hanafi
   primer's own text against later Hanafi practice on the six fasts of Shawwal, or the
   Maliki primer on a single intention for the month). A reviewer from each school should
   read its column.
2. **Unit Fasting, `kaffarah`.** Its Quran evidence is al-Mujadilah 58:3-4, the expiation
   of zihar, which the Hanafi primer points to. The fasting expiation itself rests on a
   hadith that is not in the unit. Consider adding that hadith as an evidence item.
3. **Unit Fasting, `clouded-crescent`, `intention-timing`, `delayed-qada`, `forgetting`.**
   Ibn Rushd does not name every school the claim shows; the positions rest on the primer
   quotes alone (already recorded in the unit's `review.log`).
4. **`duas-daily:leaving-home`.** Ahmad Shakir's print of al-Tirmidhi 3426 reads "hasan
   gharib"; the Sunnah.com text reads "hasan sahih gharib". We show the print's wording.
5. **`adhkar-after-prayer:anta-al-salam`.** Abd al-Baqi's print of Muslim 591 has يا ذا
   الجلال; the Sunnah.com text has ذا الجلال without يا. We follow the print.
6. **`salah-words:subhanaka`** is shown as Umar's practice (mawquf, Muslim 399), with
   al-Tirmidhi's remark that the chains traced to the Prophet ﷺ were criticised. A reviewer
   may prefer a marfu' narration with a located grading.
7. **`duas-daily:iftar`, the second wording** (Abu Dawud 2358) is mursal and is shown as a
   labelled variant, not as an item. A reviewer may prefer to remove it.
8. **`nawawi40:11`.** Sunan al-Nasa'i is cited by volume and page (8/327) and not by Abu
   Ghudda's number. The hadith stands at 5711 in hadith-api, which is one witness only, so
   the number was not added.
9. **`nawawi40:12`.** Ibn Rajab's sentence on the hadith being preserved only as mursal was
   not re-fetched in this audit (it was located by the two earlier passes).
10. **Names.** The introduction's sentence from Ibn Hajar was read only as quoted in a later
    study, not in a copy of Fath al-Bari (recorded in `names99/AUDIT.md`). "Al-Darr" and
    similar Names are explained from the verb the Quran uses; whether each derived form is
    properly a Name is itself differed on, which the introduction says in general terms.
11. **Zakat notes.** Ten quotes (Minhaj al-Talibin 4, Kifayat al-Akhyar 3, al-Muhalla 2,
    Fath al-Mu'in 1) were not re-fetched in this audit; the other 43 were. The notes
    `nisab-grams`, `basis` and `combining` stay `draft` because they rest on modern figures
    or a single witness.
12. **Hijri events.** No event carries a hadith citation yet (`citation` is empty by
    design). Ibn Rushd also records that Malik disliked singling out the white days, and
    lists the fast of Arafah among the fasts that are differed on; the descriptions do not
    say so.

Try the way every ayah is learned

This is the Memorize step of a Takrar lesson, on the first ayah of Surah Al-Ikhlas. Mishary Alafasy recites; you repeat after him, then say it from memory. Turn your sound on.

  1. 6×
    Listen and repeat, with the textHe recites, you repeat aloud in the pause.
  2. 4×
    Recite from memoryThe text is hidden. Hold to peek if you need to.
  3. 4×
    Listen againWith the text back, fixing whatever slipped.
  4. 6×
    Final recall from memoryThe version you'll carry away.
  5. ↺
    Put the words in orderA last check that you know the sequence.

In the app, ayahs are then joined one after another, and the lesson ends with the whole passage from memory. Reviews bring it back on the days that follow.

A day of hifz, planned for you

The traditional day has three parts: the new lesson (sabaq), the recent ones (sabqi) and everything older (manzil). Takrar schedules all three around the time you have.

Miss a day and nothing falls apart: a streak freeze covers it, and your reviews wait in order of need.

The new lesson

  • Listen, with each word's meaning5 min
  • Memorise: six, four, four and six15 min
  • Recite the lesson from memory3 min

The recent lessons

  • Review this week's ayahs10 min
  • Drill the ones you keep slipping on5 min

Everything older

  • Revise part of your older juz15 min

What's inside

Everything a hifz student needs, free, in one app.

Reviews that come back on time

Every ayah you learn gets its own review schedule. Recite it cleanly and it returns less often; stumble and it comes back tomorrow.

  • Your weakest ayahs gathered into a short drill
  • A map of all 604 pages by how well you hold them
  • Streak freezes for the days life gets in the way

The mushaf, page for page

Read the Madina print or the 15-line IndoPak, with every ayah where you know it. Test yourself a whole page at a time.

  • Hide the words and reveal them as you recite
  • "Ink" prints each ayah as darkly as you hold it
  • Plan a khatm and track the pages you read

Similar verses, side by side

About one ayah in eleven has a near-twin elsewhere, the commonest place to slip. Takrar shows you the pair and marks exactly where they differ.

  • One ayah turns into its twin, only the differences move
  • A quick practice for telling them apart

Recite, and it listens

Turn on the voice check and recite from memory. It follows you word by word and marks a skipped or changed word, all on your device.

  • Your recitation never leaves your phone
  • A pace line shows if you're rushing
  • Tap any coloured letter for its tajweed rule, with an example to hear

Questions

Is it really free? Yes. There are no ads, no subscription and nothing locked behind a payment.

Do I need an account? No. Everything works and is saved on your device. Sign in only if you want your progress on more than one device.

What does 6, 4, 4, 6 mean? Each new ayah is recited twenty times in four rounds: six listening and repeating with the text, four from memory, four with the text again and six from memory. Then you put its words back in order.

What are sabaq, sabqi and manzil? The three parts of the traditional hifz day: the new lesson, the recent lessons, and everything older. Takrar schedules all three and puts your weakest ayahs first.

Does the voice check upload my recitation? No. The recognition model runs on your phone or in your browser, and your voice stays there.

Which mushaf does it use? The Madina print, page for page, and the 15-line IndoPak layout. You can switch between them and keep your place.

Which reciters are there? Over twenty, including Mishary Alafasy, Mahmoud al-Husary (with a slow teaching recitation), Abdul Basit and Al-Minshawi. For most, each word lights up as it's recited.

Does it work offline? Lessons and reviews keep working once they've loaded. Download a surah's recitation to listen without a connection.

Is it only in English? For now the app, the meanings and the transliteration are in English, with two translations to choose from. More languages are planned.

What do you keep about me? As little as possible. The privacy page lists exactly what is stored and where, and you can delete your account from the app.

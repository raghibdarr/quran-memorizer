==============================================================================
Qibla compass: World Magnetic Model 2025 (NOAA NCEI and British Geological Survey)
==============================================================================

The qibla compass corrects the phone's magnetic heading to true north with the
World Magnetic Model WMM2025 (released 13 November 2024), produced by the US National Oceanic and
Atmospheric Administration's National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) and the British
Geological Survey. Takrar evaluates NOAA's published coefficient file on your device with its own
code; nothing is sent anywhere. The model is a work of the US government and is in the public
domain. https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/world-magnetic-model

The qibla bearing comes from adhan (above). The sun's position for the sun aid is worked out on
your device with Takrar's own implementation of Jean Meeus's published formulas (Astronomical
Algorithms, 2nd edition, 1998).

Hijri dates come from your device's own Umm al-Qura calendar, or from Takrar's arithmetic

Mosque lists: OpenStreetMap
==============================================================================

Data © OpenStreetMap contributors (https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright), available under the
Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0: https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/

The mosque lists in the app (data/mosques/<country>.json) are a Derivative Database made from
OpenStreetMap features tagged amenity=place_of_worship and religion=muslim: each mosque's map
position, rounded, and its name. They are offered under the same licence. The files, and the
script that builds them (scripts/build-mosques.mjs), are public in the app's source repository,
so anyone can get, rebuild and reuse them. The app loads one country's list when you ask for it,
sorts it by distance on your device, and never sends your position anywhere.

Country assignment at build time used Natural Earth 1:10m admin-0 countries (public domain,
https://www.naturalearthdata.com); the boundaries themselves are not included in the app.

==============================================================================
Adhkar and duas
==============================================================================

The adhkar and duas decks (morning and evening, after the prayer, sleeping and waking, duas for
the day, the words of the prayer) quote hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi
Dawud, Jami' al-Tirmidhi and Sunan Ibn Majah: classical works in the public domain. The Arabic
is transcribed and vowelled by Takrar from the printed editions as al-Maktaba al-Shamila
(shamela.ws) gives them, and collated against fawazahmed0/hadith-api for comparison only.
Gradings quote al-Tirmidhi's Jami', al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar (read in the al-Arna'ut edition on
shamela.ws) and al-Daraqutni's Sunan; each is attributed by name on the item. The English
translations are Takrar's own work. Nothing from Hisn al-Muslim, Sunnah.com's English,
Darussalam or any published translation is used. Quranic items are references only and render
from the app's licensed Quran text.


==============================================================================
Names of Allah
==============================================================================

The meanings, explanations, transliterations, intro text and glossary entry of the Names of
Allah deck are Takrar's own work. The transliterations are made by Takrar's own scheme
(src/lib/transliterate.ts) from our own vocalised Arabic of each Name.

The order of the Names follows the enumeration narrated in the Jami' of al-Tirmidhi
(d. 279 AH), a public-domain work; the Names themselves are not anyone's text. Quran
references point to the Tanzil Quran text already bundled with the app (listed above); no
Quran text is stored in the deck beyond the single word or phrase a derived Name is taken
from, copied from that same Tanzil text. Hadith and works of scholarship (Sahih al-Bukhari,
Sahih Muslim, al-Tirmidhi's Jami', al-Tabari's Jami' al-Bayan, Ibn Hajar's Fath al-Bari,
Ibn Kathir's Tafsir) are cited, not reproduced.

Online copies were read only to confirm the list, the citation numbers and the scholars'
remarks (surahquran.com, hadithanswers.com, dorar.net, page views of sunnah.com, the
hadith-api Arabic edition, the tafsir pages of quran.ksu.edu.sa, shamela.ws). Nothing from
those sites is bundled.


==============================================================================
Foundations course, Unit Purification
==============================================================================

The lessons, recall cards, check questions and glossary definitions of the purification
unit are Takrar's own work, written for this app.

Each school's position is cited, by work and chapter, to one classical primer, with a short
phrase of its Arabic quoted as the words relied on:
  Hanafi:   Mukhtasar al-Quduri, by Abu al-Husayn al-Quduri (d. 428 AH)
  Maliki:   al-Risala, by Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani (d. 386 AH)
  Shafi'i:  Matn Abi Shuja' (al-Ghaya wa al-Taqrib), by Abu Shuja' al-Asfahani (d. 593 AH)
  Hanbali:  'Umdat al-Fiqh, by Ibn Qudamah al-Maqdisi (d. 620 AH)
Points of agreement and difference are cited to Bidayat al-Mujtahid by Ibn Rushd (d. 595 AH),
al-Ijma' by Ibn al-Mundhir (d. 318 AH) and Maratib al-Ijma' by Ibn Hazm (d. 456 AH).

These works are in the public domain. They are cited and briefly quoted, not reproduced; no
edition's notes, introduction or apparatus is used. The quotations were read in the copies at
shamela.ws (al-Maktaba al-Shamila), page by page, as recorded in
content/deen/course/purification/citations.json. Quran references are rendered from the
app's own Quran text.

Status: source-checked by the authoring pipeline. Not yet reviewed by a scholar.


==============================================================================
Course, Unit Fasting
==============================================================================

The lessons, cards, questions, glossary entries and English wording of Unit Fasting are
Takrar's own work.

School positions are cited, by work and chapter with a short Arabic phrase, from four
classical primers, all long in the public domain: Mukhtasar al-Quduri (al-Quduri, d. 428 AH),
al-Risala (Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani, d. 386 AH), Matn Abi Shuja' (Abu Shuja' al-Asfahani,
d. 593 AH) and 'Umdat al-Fiqh (Ibn Qudamah, d. 620 AH). Agreement and difference are cited
from Bidayat al-Mujtahid (Ibn Rushd, d. 595 AH) and al-Ijma' (Ibn al-Mundhir, d. 318 AH),
also public domain. These works are cited, not reproduced.

Three hadith are quoted in Arabic from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sunan Abi Dawud (public-domain
texts, our own transcription collated against two witnesses) with our own English translation.
One grading is quoted, with attribution, from the Sunan of al-Daraqutni (d. 385 AH), and a remark
of al-Nawawi (d. 676 AH) in al-Adhkar is paraphrased with attribution; both works are public domain.
Quran passages are shown from the app's licensed Quran text and translation, listed above.

Online copies were read to locate and check the wording (shamela.ws, dorar.net, hadith-api,
and the OpenITI text of al-Adhkar).
No text, translation or commentary from those sites is bundled.
